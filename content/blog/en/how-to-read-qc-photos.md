QC photos exist so you can inspect an item before it ships. The problem is that most buyers look at them the same way they look at a product listing — checking that it looks right rather than checking whether it *is* right.

Here is what to look for, and what to ignore.

## First: understand what the photos can and cannot show

A QC set is usually 6–12 phone photos under mixed lighting. That means:

**They can show:** shape and proportions, stitching consistency, logo placement, colour, sole pattern, hardware finish, packaging and labels.

**They cannot show:** material feel, weight, smell, long-term durability, or whether an internal component is correct.

Do not try to judge what the camera cannot capture. Judge what it can, thoroughly.

## The lighting trap

Almost every QC complaint that turns out to be nothing is a lighting artefact.

- **Hard shadows** make stitching look uneven and colours look wrong.
- **Warm indoor light** shifts grey towards brown and makes white look cream.
- **Phone HDR** flattens contrast, so dark-on-dark details disappear.

Before flagging anything, ask: could this be the light? Rotate the photo, look at the shadow direction, and compare across multiple shots of the same area.

## Checklist: sneakers

**Shape and proportions.** Compare the toe box height, the angle of the heel, and the height of the collar against a reference photo of the same model. Proportion is the hardest thing to fix and the most common weak point.

**Stitching.** Follow one seam from start to finish. Stitch length should be even, the line should stay parallel to the panel edge, and there should be no skipped stitches or loose thread ends.

**Panel alignment.** On a multi-panel shoe, check where panels meet. Consistent gaps, no overlapping edges, no puckering.

**Logos and branding.** Position matters more than size. A logo 2 mm off centre is a more reliable signal than a logo that is slightly the wrong weight.

**Sole and outsole.** Look at the tread pattern depth and the moulding quality at the edges. Cheap soles have rounded, soft edges; good ones are sharp.

**Midsole paint.** On models with painted midsoles, check for overspray onto the upper and for uneven coverage near the sole line.

## Checklist: bags

**Hardware.** Zips, clasps, D-rings. They should look solid, with clean edges and consistent plating colour. Look for the tell-tale orange peel texture that indicates cheap electroplating.

**Edge paint.** Follow the painted edge around a corner. It should be smooth and even in thickness. Bubbles, drips and thin patches are the most common flaw.

**Stitch density.** Count stitches per inch on a straight run. Higher density costs more to produce and is a decent proxy for overall quality.

**Monogram alignment.** On patterned leather, the pattern should be centred and symmetric across the seam. Misalignment is visible and almost impossible to fix later.

**Interior lining.** Ask for an interior photo. It is rarely provided and it is where corners are most often cut.

## Checklist: watches

**Dial print.** Zoom in. Text edges should be sharp, spacing even, and the print centred.

**Bezel alignment.** The 12 o'clock marker on the bezel should line up with the dial marker. This is a one-glance check.

**Date window.** If present, the date should sit centred in the window at 12, with clean cut edges.

**Bracelet end links.** The fit between bracelet and lugs should be tight, with no visible gap when viewed from the side.

**Hands.** Check the length — hour hand should reach the inner edge of the indices, minute hand should reach the minute track.

## How to grade what you find

Not every flaw is a rejection. Sort them:

| Grade | Example | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Photo artefact** | Shadow across stitching | Ignore |
| **Cosmetic, hidden** | Slight mark inside the tongue | Acceptable |
| **Cosmetic, visible** | Off-centre logo on the outer side | Ask for a replacement |
| **Structural** | Uneven sole, misaligned panels | Reject |

Be specific when you raise an issue. "Stitching looks bad" gets you a generic reply; "left shoe, outer seam, three skipped stitches near the heel, second photo" gets you a replacement.

## What good QC practice looks like

The most useful thing you can do is ask for the specific angle you need rather than accepting the standard set. Reputable sellers will send additional photos of a specific area — heel shape from behind, interior lining, sole from underneath — because they know it settles the question faster than an argument later.

If you want to compare against references while you review, browsing by model helps: [Air Jordan](/c/air-jordan) and [Nike](/c/nike) pages group similar silhouettes together, which makes proportion differences much easier to spot than looking at a single pair in isolation.
