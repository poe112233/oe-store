Leather does not wear out from use. It wears out from three things: moisture loss, abrasion, and bad storage. Address those and a well-made bag will outlast the trend that made you buy it.

## What damages leather

**Drying.** Leather is skin. Without conditioning, the oils migrate and the surface cracks — usually first at the folds and along the handle roots. This is the number one killer, and it is entirely preventable.

**Abrasion.** Corners and edges rub against everything. Once the surface layer is gone, it does not come back; it can only be dyed over.

**Water and heat.** Rain leaves water marks. Radiators and car boots bake moisture out. Both cause the same damage as drying, just faster.

**Mould from storage.** A bag sealed in a plastic bag in a humid room will grow mould on the lining and under the flap. Mould stains permanently.

## The four-product routine

You do not need a shelf of products. You need four:

1. **Soft horsehair brush** — for dust. Never use a cloth on dry leather; it grinds dust into the grain.
2. **pH-neutral leather cleaner** — for surface grime, used sparingly.
3. **Leather conditioner / cream** — the important one. Replenishes oils.
4. **Cotton dust bag** — for storage. Not plastic.

## The routine

**Weekly (30 seconds).** Empty the bag, brush out the interior, wipe the exterior with a dry cloth. Dust is abrasive; removing it is 80% of care.

**Monthly (5 minutes).** Apply a small amount of conditioner with a soft cloth, working in circles. Use less than you think — a pea-sized amount per panel. Let it absorb for 15 minutes, then buff off the excess. If the leather still feels tacky, you used too much.

**Seasonally (15 minutes).** Full clean: brush, clean with pH-neutral cleaner on a damp cloth, let dry naturally, then condition. Do this at the start and end of the seasons you use the bag most.

**Annually.** Check the hardware, the stitching at stress points, and the corners. Address small problems before they become repairs.

## Specific problems

**Scratches.** On smooth leather, light scratches often disappear with a fingertip's warmth and a little conditioner. Deep scratches need matching cream polish — apply in thin layers, let dry between.

**Corner wear.** The first sign is a lighter patch where the finish has rubbed. Condition immediately to slow it, and consider a leather restorer for colour. Once the corner is down to the fibre, only a professional repair helps.

**Water marks.** Blot immediately, do not rub. Let it dry away from heat. A water mark that has set can sometimes be lifted by dampening the whole panel evenly and drying slowly — but this is risky and worth practising on an inconspicuous area.

**Handle darkening.** Handles darken from hand oils, which is normal and generally considered patina. If it bothers you, clean the handles more often than the body.

**Mould.** Wipe with a dilute white vinegar solution (1:1 with water), dry thoroughly in indirect air, then condition. Do not put it away until it is completely dry.

## Storage

This is where most damage happens, and where most people stop trying.

- **Stuff the bag** with acid-free tissue or a clean cloth so it holds its shape.
- **Stand it upright** — never stack bags on top of each other.
- **Use the dust bag**, not plastic.
- **Keep it out of direct sun** and away from radiators.
- **Leave the flap open slightly** if the bag is stored for months, so the leather does not take a permanent crease.

## What not to do

- Do not use baby wipes. They contain alcohol and surfactants that strip oils.
- Do not use saddle soap on finished leather; it is for raw leather.
- Do not dry with a hairdryer. Ever.
- Do not store in a plastic bag.
- Do not over-condition. Once a month is plenty; more softens the structure.

## The honest expectation

A leather bag cared for this way will show use — creases at the folds, a slight shine on the handles, softened structure. That is what good leather does. The goal is not to keep it looking new; it is to keep it from cracking, tearing or growing mould. Those are the failures that cannot be undone.
