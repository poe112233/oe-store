Stitching is the most honest thing on any garment, bag or shoe. It cannot be faked cheaply, it degrades visibly, and it reflects how the whole item was assembled. Learning to read a seam takes about five minutes and pays off on everything you buy.

## The four things to check

### 1. Stitch density

Count stitches per inch (SPI) on a straight run.

| SPI | Quality |
|---|---|
| 4–5 | Fast fashion. Weak, will pull apart under load. |
| 6–8 | Standard commercial. Acceptable for non-stress areas. |
| 9–12 | Good quality. Typical of well-made bags and shoes. |
| 13+ | Fine work. Usually hand-finished or high-end machine. |

Higher density costs more because it takes longer, uses more thread, and requires better machines. That is exactly why it is a useful proxy for overall quality.

### 2. Tension

Tension is how tightly the thread is pulled. On a balanced stitch, the threads interlock inside the material and are invisible from both sides.

Signs of bad tension:

- **Loops on the surface** — the top thread is too loose or the bobbin is wrong.
- **Puckering** — the material is drawn together along the seam.
- **Visible thread from the other side** — the knot has migrated to the surface.

Puckering is the most reliable signal. It means the seam is under strain and will fail at the weakest point.

### 3. Alignment

Follow a seam from start to finish and watch the distance to the edge. It should stay constant within a millimetre.

- **Wandering** — the operator was fighting the material. Indicates speed over accuracy.
- **Converging then diverging** — panels were mismatched and forced.
- **Inconsistent seam allowance** — the item was not cut accurately to begin with.

### 4. Thread type

- **Cotton** — traditional, ages well, breaks before the leather does. Good sign.
- **Polyester** — strong, UV resistant, standard on most modern goods. Fine.
- **Bonded nylon** — very strong, used on heavy leather goods. Good sign.
- **Fuzzy, thick, uniform thread** — often indicates a cheap synthetic chosen for appearance rather than strength.

Look at the thread ends. A good seam is backstitched and the tail is trimmed or burned; a cheap one is left loose or glued.

## Where to look first

You do not need to inspect everything. Check these in order:

1. **Stress points.** Where a strap meets a bag body, where a handle attaches, where a sole meets an upper. These fail first, so quality here is not accidental.
2. **The longest straight seam.** Easiest place to spot wandering and inconsistent density.
3. **The inside.** Interior seams are where corners get cut. If the inside is neat, the maker cared.
4. **Curves.** Sewing a tight curve well requires skill. Look for skipped stitches and pleating on the inside of the curve.

## What flaws actually mean

Not every flaw is disqualifying. Sort them by consequence:

| Flaw | Meaning | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 skipped stitches on a hidden seam | Operator error | Usually fine |
| Skipped stitches at a stress point | Will fail under load | Reject |
| Slight density variation | Minor inconsistency | Usually fine |
| Puckering along a long seam | Tension problem, will fail | Reject |
| Loose thread ends | Finishing shortcut | Trim and move on |
| Glued-instead-of-stitched join | Structural shortcut | Reject |

## The thirty-second test

Pick up the item and do this:

1. Find the longest straight seam. Count ten stitches, estimate SPI.
2. Run your eye along that seam to the end. Watch for wandering or puckering.
3. Turn it over. Look at the reverse. Is the stitch clean on both sides?
4. Go to a stress point. Is it reinforced — double-stitched, bar-tacked, or riveted?

Four checks, thirty seconds. It will not tell you everything, but it separates honest construction from cosmetic construction faster than anything else.

## Why this matters more than materials

Materials are visible and easily described. Stitching is invisible to most buyers and expensive to get right — which makes it a much better signal. An item with excellent stitching and average leather will outlast an item with premium leather and rushed construction, every time.
